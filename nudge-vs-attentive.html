Nudge vs Attentive: enterprise SMS without the platform minimum

The short version: Attentive is purpose-built for e-commerce marketing SMS — abandoned-cart messages, promotions, drip campaigns. Nudge is purpose-built for billing notifications, payment reminders, and operational alerts in regulated industries. Nudge has no platform minimum and bundles email, WhatsApp, voice, and push in the same subscription. Different jobs, different fits.

Bottom line:

Attentive is excellent if you're a Shopify-style retailer running abandoned-cart and post-purchase SMS. If you're sending billing notifications, payment reminders, outage alerts, or OTP for a regulated business — Nudge has no platform minimum and ships with the compliance and templates Attentive's e-commerce focus doesn't cover.

Pricing structure

Attentive's pricing model layers a per-message rate on top of a monthly platform fee. Even at low send volumes, the platform fee applies. Nudge has no platform minimum — you pay only for what you send, regardless of channel.

Multi-channel coverage

Attentive is SMS-first. To match Nudge's email + SMS + WhatsApp coverage, an Attentive stack typically pulls in SendGrid for email and Twilio Conversations for WhatsApp — three vendors instead of one.

Frequently asked: Nudge vs Attentive

How does Nudge pricing compare to Attentive?

Attentive's pricing model uses per-message rates plus a monthly platform minimum. Nudge has no platform minimum and bundles SMS with email, WhatsApp, voice, and push notifications in one subscription. Nudge pricing is finalized per organization based on volume and channel mix — contact sales or use the ROI calculator for a tailored quote.

Does Attentive charge a platform minimum?

Yes. Attentive's pricing model includes a monthly platform fee on top of per-message charges. Nudge has no platform minimum — you pay only for the volumes you send, regardless of channel mix.

Is Attentive built for e-commerce or for utility-style billing?

Attentive is built primarily for e-commerce SMS marketing — abandoned-cart messages, promotions, drip campaigns. Nudge is built for transactional and notification SMS — billing alerts, payment reminders, outage notifications, OTP — for utilities, healthcare, insurance, and financial services. The two products solve different problems.

Can Nudge replace Attentive for SMS marketing campaigns?

Yes for most campaigns. Nudge supports segmentation, drip flows, A/B testing, and broadcast SMS. Attentive's strength is its e-commerce conversion-optimization tooling (abandoned-cart, browse-abandonment, post-purchase) which is specific to retail. If you're a regulated-industry brand sending billing or service notifications via SMS, Nudge is a better fit.

Does Nudge support 2-way SMS, short codes, and toll-free?

Yes. Nudge supports 2-way messaging, short codes, toll-free SMS numbers, MMS, and conversational AI auto-responses out of the box, at parity with Attentive.

What about email, WhatsApp, and voice — does Attentive offer those?

Attentive launched email and direct mail products in recent years but its core remains SMS marketing. It does not offer WhatsApp Business, voice/IVR, or push notifications. Nudge includes all of those channels in the same plan as SMS.

Is Nudge TCPA-compliant for SMS marketing?

Yes. Nudge ships with TCPA and FDCPA tooling for SMS — opt-in management, quiet-hours enforcement, double-opt-in flows, and STOP/HELP keyword handling. Both Nudge and Attentive support TCPA-compliant patterns; Attentive's marketing-first focus means more retail-style consent flows, while Nudge supports billing and collections-style patterns natively.
